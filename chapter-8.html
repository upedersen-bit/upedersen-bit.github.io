Chapter 8

The children and the legacy

The times: a rising countryside, 1870–1920s. The Denmark his children grew up in changed fast. After 1870, cheap American and Russian grain pushed Danish farmers toward livestock. The first cooperative dairy opened in 1882, there were 1,157 by 1909, and butter and bacon became the great exports to Britain. The cooperatives grew alongside the folk high school movement.1 Tuberculosis was called "the robber of youth" for its toll on young adults across Europe, and it took Ulrica at 29.2 About 390,000 Danes emigrated in the century before 1914.3 Scandinavians settled New Zealand too; Danes, Norwegians and Swedes founded Dannevirke there in 1872. That wider story lies behind a Nebraska inheritance that ended as a piano shipped to New Zealand.4

His Four Children

He left four children in Denmark, aged about 2 to 11. The oldest two rose further than their father had. Their great-uncle Anders Stephansen seems to have helped.5

Ulrikka. Ulrica, who spelled her name Ulrikka as an adult, was the youngest of the four and the one through whom this biography's compilers descend. She was born at Terndrup on 24 March 1863, and at her baptism her father was entered as "Indsidder Niels Christian Olesen Damborg": the first time the name appears. She was three when he left for America and eleven when her mother died. She was then sent across the Kattegat to Holsteinsminde near Næstved on Zealand, the children's home her great-uncle Anders Stephansen had founded, about 200 km from Terndrup. She was confirmed there in 1877.9

She stayed in that corner of Zealand. On 10 December 1881, aged 18, she married Peter Nielsen in Tystrup church, a few kilometres from Holsteinsminde. He was 29, the son of the smallholder Niels Petersen of Vindstrup Hestehave in Tystrup parish, and became a carpenter and smallholder there himself. The register describes her as the daughter of the "former lodger Niels Christian Olsen Damborg of Terndrup", and a partly illegible note beside the entry records the consent of her guardian in Terndrup, since she was under age. Her mother's probate of 1880 names Thomas Nielsen of Terndrup as her guardian. They had three children: Hans, born 23 December 1882; Kirsten Marie, named after Ulrikka's mother, born 25 October 1884; and Lovise Jørgine, born 19 July 1888.9

The marriage lasted eleven years. Peter Nielsen died on 20 August 1892. Six months later, on 18 February 1893, Ulrikka died at Vindstrup Hestehave of lungesyge, lung disease, probably tuberculosis. She was 29. The priest who buried her at Tystrup on 24 February entered her father once more as Niels Christian Olesen Damborg of Terndrup. Her three children, then aged ten, eight and four, were orphans. Kirsten Marie died at Vindstrup Hestehave in 1896, aged eleven. Lovise grew up, married Carl Gustav Pedersen, and lived at Vindstrup until her death on 15 August 1964. Through her son Erik Johannes Pedersen (1911–1985) and his son Leo Erik Ove Damborg Pedersen (born 1942), the line runs to Ulrik Damborg, who compiled this biography with his father.9

The Inheritance

The family story says the emigrant did not forget his Danish children entirely. His estate was divided among them, with the final settlement in 1922. His granddaughter Lovise Nielsen, Ulrikka's daughter, used her share to buy a piano. She later shipped it to her own daughter, Ester, in New Zealand. The name Damborg, which he took up in 1863 and dropped when he left, is today the surname of his descendants.10

A letter in the Viborg provincial archive shows the family at work on the inheritance. On 29 January 1918 Anders Christian, then a teacher at Lekkende near Allerslev on Zealand, wrote to the archive asking urgently for his father's birth certificate and for his grandparents' certificates of birth, marriage and death. These, he wrote, were "af den allerstørste Betydning" to him, "of the very greatest importance". He gave his father's name as Niels Chr. Olsen, born in 1821, probably in the summer. The year 1822 in the family's papers came from the War Ministry's certificate of his war service.

The archivist found the Als baptism of 1 June 1821, the confirmation at Skelund in 1835, the parents Ole Nielsen and Maren Jensdatter of Hurup, and the marriage at Lyngby on 22 July 1854. In its reply of 31 January 1918 the archive added that his grandparents had married in Mou church in February 1821, both aged 37, when Ole Nielsen was in service at "Hovna", probably Havnø. It could not find where they came from or when they died, and it noted that the Visborg church books before 1814 had burned. The timing fits the American estate. If the widow, Mary, died in 1917, the Nebraska property would then pass to the Danish children, who had to prove their descent. That makes it more likely that the Mary N. Olson who died in Washington County in 1917 was his widow.10
