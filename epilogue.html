Epilogue

A life in two halves

Niels Christian Olesen lived two lives of almost equal length. For forty-five years he was a Dane of the poorest rural class: a lodger's son raised by foster parents, a farmhand, a cavalryman in the war of 1848–50, and a son-in-law with no land of his own at Terndrup. For thirty-three years he was Nelse C. Olson, a Nebraska homesteader who held his land by patent, prospered modestly, married again and was buried beside New York Creek among Norwegian neighbours.

The break between the two halves was hard on those he left behind. Kirsten Marie kept the farm going with her mother and died at 49. The children grew up without him, and Ulrica was raised in her great-uncle's children's home. Yet both his sons rose further than he had, and, as the family tells it, his Nebraska estate came back to his Danish children in the end.

Much is still unknown:

  • whether he crossed on The Queen to New York in May 1866 or on the Moravian to Quebec in June 1866;
  • whether a Danish court ever dissolved his first marriage before he married again in 1871;
  • who his father was and when he died, and where the name Damborg came from: Ole Nielsen Damborg of Gravlev, who died in 1836, now seems less likely, since Niels Christian's mother was probably a widow by 1830;
  • who Mary Nelsen was, when she died, and why his estate was not settled until 1922.
