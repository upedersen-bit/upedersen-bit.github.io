Appendix C

Conflicts in the tree and open questions

Writing the biography turned up several places where the tree contradicts itself. These are worth fixing on Ancestry or researching further.2

Conflicts to fix

  • Death year. His death is recorded as 21 Nov 1899, and the grave photo is dated 1899. A note on the same fact says "at his death in 1915" he left his belongings to his children, with final settlement in 1922. The biography uses 1899. Resolved: his Find a Grave memorial (N. C. Olson, Thone Cemetery) confirms 21 Nov 1899. The 1915 date may be close to when his widow, Mary, died. She was still alive in the 1910 census, and a Mary N. Olson, probably her, died in Washington County in 1917. If she kept the farm for her lifetime, that would explain why the estate passed to his Danish children only later, with settlement in 1922. This is a hypothesis to test in the Washington County probate records.
  • Anders Stephansen and Holsteinsminde. The tree gives his birth as 20 May 1790 and calls Holsteinsminde a school for girls. Dansk Biografisk Leksikon gives 1791 (20 May; a 1941 memorial book gives 20 August) and describes Holsteinsminde as a home for poor and neglected children of both sexes.3
  • Death place. The place field says "Thone Cemetery, Herman". That is where he is buried, so it belongs in a Burial fact. His place of death is not recorded.
  • Birthplace. The birth fact says Lyngby Sogn, Hindsted Herred. Resolved: the Als baptism register places the family at Hurup in Als Sogn. Lyngby is where he married later, and it lies in Hellum Herred, not Hindsted. The baptism fact should also read 2 June (at home), with 8 July as the church presentation.
  • Confirmation parish. The fact says Fleskum Sogn, but the image title and web link say Skelund Sogn.
  • Two mothers. He is linked to two families with the same father: Maren Jensdatter (b. 1805, Rold) and Maren Jensen (b. 1778, no source given), marked as adoptive mother. These are the two competing candidates for his mother. The duplicate family should be merged or clearly labelled as an alternative theory. Resolved: Tre Slægter fra Rold lists the 1805 Maren Jensdatter as unmarried, and she died at Rold in 1879, so she is not his mother. The tree's death date for his mother, 23 February 1879, belongs to her and should be removed. The tree's 1778 should also be corrected: the bride of 1821 was 37 and the Skelund wife of 1834 was 51, which put her birth about 1783.
  • Farm name. Kirsten Marie's note says "Sjølundholm". Jens Sørensen's census link says "Sjøvadsholm".
  • Year of the name change. The name-change fact is dated 1862, but Ulrica, at whose baptism the name first appears, was born on 24 March 1863. Another note says 1861.
  • Homestead description. "NW quarter of the SW quarter of the NW quarter of Section 10" describes 10 acres, not 80. Resolved: the patent reads NW¼ of the SW¼ and SW¼ of the NW¼, two 40-acre tracts. The fact in the tree should be corrected.
  • Date of his divorce letter. The tree titles the image "1870 NCO ask for divorce", but the letter carries the same archive mark (D 1092-69) as Kirsten Marie's petition and was enclosed with it, so it must date from 1869 or earlier.
  • 1860 household. The 1860 fact lists "Ole Nielsen (5 yrs)" and "Ole Nielsen (63 yrs)". Resolved: the census image is from Rybjerg Sogn, Nørre Herred, Viborg Amt, and shows a different Niels Christian Olesen, aged 32 and born in that parish, with a wife also named Kirsten Marie Jensen and sons Ole (5) and Jens (3). It should be detached from him. The family's own 1860 entry has now been found, at Sjøvadsholm in Lyngby Sogn (see chapter 3).

Open questions

  1. Who was his mother? Answered in part: not the Damborg descendant born in 1805, who died unmarried in 1879. The records point to a Maren Jensdatter born about 1783, who married Christian Frederich Christensen in 1830 and lived in Skelund in 1834. Where and when she died is not yet known. The 1860 census confirms that the 1805 Maren was single: she was 55 and unmarried, living at Rold with her widowed mother in the household of her brother Peder Jensen Damborg. See Appendix E. To find where his mother was born, look for Christian Frederich Christensen's household in the 1845 Skelund census, which gives birthplaces, and in the Visborg and Mou moving-in lists (tilgangslister) for about 1819–21, as the Viborg archivist advised in 1918.
  2. Who was his father, and when did he die? Maren was probably entered as a widow (Enken) when she remarried on 25 September 1830, so look first for Ole Nielsen's burial between about 1824 and 1830 (Skelund, Als, Visborg, Hadsund and neighbouring parishes), and for his death or probate in the Hindsted herred registers (dødsanmeldelsesprotokol, skifteprotokol). The parish clerk's copy of the Skelund register (kontraministerialbog) may give a clearer reading of her status. If no death is found, look for a divorce or desertion licence (skilsmissebevilling) for Ole Nielsen and Maren Jensdatter, about 1824–30, in the Aalborg amt records: only then could Ole Nielsen Damborg of Gravlev (d. 1836) be his father. For the Gravlev man himself, look for his burial in neighbouring parishes (Buderup, Aarestrup, Rold, Skørping) and his age in the 1834 census: about 50 would fit; about 93 would make him the Ole Nielsen Damborg born in 1742. Since he was a pauper (fattiglem), Gravlev's poor-relief records (fattigprotokol), which often give a pauper's age, may also show who he was.
  3. What went wrong in 1863–66? Check the Hellum-Hindsted district court's mortgage records (panteprotokol), police records and justice records.
  4. Was a divorce granted? Kirsten Marie's letter of 23 May 1869 shows the case heading for court. Look for the judgment in the Hellum-Hindsted district court records and in the Aalborg Stiftamt's journal for 1869 (D 1092-69). This now matters more: he married in Nebraska on 23 February 1868 and again on 20 February 1871, both times while Kirsten Marie was alive.
  5. Who was Mary Nelsen? Answered in part: the 1871 marriage licence shows that Caren (1880) and Mary C. (1885) were almost certainly the same woman, born about 1836 at Kongsberg, Norway, daughter of Nils Severson and Emily Thorbien [?]. The 1910 census shows her still living in Grant Township as a widow of 80 who had come to America in 1867. Search the Kongsberg church books (about 1829–37) and the Norwegian emigrant records for 1867 for her, and her death certificate: the Nebraska death index lists a Mary N. Olson, Washington County, 1917, certificate no. 2263, who is probably her.
  6. When exactly did he emigrate, and on what ship? Two candidates (see chapter 6): a "Niels Olsen", 44, labourer, who arrived at New York on 2 May 1866 aboard The Queen from Liverpool (passenger no. 884), and a "Nils Olsen", 46, who landed at Quebec in June 1866 from the Moravian. Neither list gives a home parish. Look at the passengers listed near each man for others from Himmerland, and for any of them later found near him in Nebraska.
  7. When did he file his homestead entry at the West Point land office? The homestead case file at the National Archives would name the filing date and his witnesses. Ordered from the National Archives (NATF 84: West Point land office, final certificate no. 651) on 1 October 2026; expected in 60–90 days.
  8. Is there a naturalization record or US probate file for Nelse C. Olson in Washington County? The 1870 census marks him as a US citizen over 21, so look for a declaration of intention filed between 1866 and 1870.
  9. What was his rank, and where did the 5th Dragoon Regiment fight in 1848–50? The c. 1849 roll page names his regiment and his start date, 1 April 1844. The regiment's muster rolls (mandtalsruller) at Rigsarkivet should give his squadron, rank and discharge date. Record the archive reference for the c. 1849 roll page as well.
  10. What is in the Hellum-Hindsted probate register (skifteprotokol), archive reference B41-5342? Check it for the probates of Jens Sørensen (1865) and Kirsten Marie Jensen (1874). Answered in part: her estate was settled on 28 September 1880 (pp. 200–201). The heirs were her four children, with Thomas Nielsen of Terndrup as Ulrikke's guardian, and each share came to about 803 kroner [?]. Jens Sørensen's probate has still to be checked.
  11. Who acquired his farm, and when? The 1908 plat book still shows 120 acres in Section 10 as "N. C. Olsen". Search the Washington County Register of Deeds numerical (tract) index for the W½ of Section 10, T19N R10E, from 1899 to about 1925, and the county court probate files for Nelse C. Olson (1899), Mary Olson (1917) and Bathilda Nielsen. A deed from about 1917–22 may name the Danish heirs.
  12. Who was Anna Christiana Oleson? On 19 February 1868 the Washington County probate judge licensed the marriage of "Nels Christian Oleson, aged 46 years" and "Anna Christiana Oleson, aged 59 years", both of the county, and a minister married them on 23 February 1868. In the July 1870 census he is listed alone. Look for her death, burial or a divorce in Washington County, 1868–70. This marriage took place before he asked Kirsten Marie for a divorce, so the question of bigamy begins in 1868, not 1871.
  13. Was the Peder Jacobsen of Terndrup who stood godfather to Jacob Jensen Damborg's son at Lyngby in March 1856 the same P. Jacobsen who managed the family's affairs from 1865 to 1880? If so, one man was close to both families before Niels Christian took the name. Check the 1855 and 1860 Terndrup census entries for him, and whether he was related to the Damborgs.
  14. Where did he work between his confirmation in 1835 and the army in 1844, and before he came to Terndrup in 1853? The 1840 and 1845 censuses, Lyngby's moving-in list for 1853 and the 1855 census would show it. Service near a Damborg household would be the simplest explanation of the name.
  15. Were Anders Olesen (sponsor at Anders Christian's baptism in 1854) and Jens Olesen (sponsor at Ulrica's in 1863), both of Terndrup, related to him?
  16. Who witnessed his letter from New York Creek? The witness lines read "P. Jacobsen [?]" and "Jens Sørensen". His father-in-law Jens Sørensen died in July 1865, so this was another man, or the names were added in Denmark.
  17. Where was his foster mother, Karen Nielsdatter, born, and when did she die? His foster father, Christen Nielsen, was born in Skelund about 1771 and was a widower by 1845. Look for her burial in Skelund, 1834–45.
  18. Who held the tenancy of Jens Sørensen's farm, and who took it over after his death in July 1865? Answered in part: the 1840 census lists him as a fæstehuusmand at no. 14, so he held it on lease. The landowner has still to be identified; the landowner's tenancy register (fæsteprotokol) should hold his fæstebrev and show whether the farm passed to his widow or daughter rather than to Niels Christian.
  19. Was he the Niels Christian Olesen who left Lillebrøndum in Bælum parish for Viborg in 1841, and for Terndrup in 1842, and who arrived back at Lillebrøndum from Dalsgaard in November 1847 (chapter 2)? Was Dalsgaard the manor of that name in Vive parish, and does Vive's moving-out list for 1847 give his birthplace? Check Lyngby's arrivals list (tilgangsliste) for 1842–43 for his arrival at Terndrup and his birthplace, Viborg's arrivals list for 1841–42, and Bælum's arrivals lists for the late 1830s to see when he came to Lillebrøndum and from where. Was he also the Niels Christian Olesen who arrived in Rold parish in December 1844? Probably not, since that man was 20 in 1844, three years younger than him. A parish arrivals list (tilgangsliste), indexed by FamilySearch as Rold (record QGD6-2KVP), enters under 2 December 1844, no. 18: "Niels Christian Olesen, 20, Tjenestekarl", come from Aalborg (index register fol. 103). The name matches, but he was 23 in December 1844 and had joined the 5th Dragoons on 1 April 1844. If it is him, it is the only record that places him near Damborg. Check the Rold moving-out lists (afgangslister) for 1845–53 and the Aalborg moving-out lists for late 1844 for a birthplace, and confirm the parish and film details of the FamilySearch record.
