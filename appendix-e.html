Appendix E

The two Marens

Two women named Maren Jensdatter have been taken for Niels Christian's mother. The records show that his mother was born about 1783 and married twice. The other woman, the Maren of the Damborg family of Rold, was born in 1805 and never married. This appendix sets their lives side by side and gives the reasons the Rold Maren was set aside.

How the two were joined

The family tree gives his mother a death at Rold on 23 February 1879, which is the Rold Maren's burial. Once that date was attached, the tree linked his mother to the Damborg family, and the name he later took seemed to come with her. Tre Slægter fra Rold lists a Maren among Jens Jakobsen Damborg's children, which made the link look sourced.

The name itself invites confusion. Maren Jensdatter was one of the commonest women's names in Himmerland. The Visborg marriage page of 1821 has two brides of that name, and the search for his mother turned up others: a married couple Ole Nielsen, 34, and Maren Jensdatter, 29, at Store Brøndum in 1834, with a daughter aged 4; and a widow Maren Jensdatter, 62, on poor relief at Rold in 1840. Some recent family notes also place the Rold Maren in Niels Christian's household at Damborg. No record supports this.

Why the Rold Maren was set aside

  1. She was too young. The bride of 1821 was 37, the lodger who arrived in Skelund in 1824 was 40, and the Skelund wife of 1834 was 51, all pointing to a birth about 1783–84. The 1821 ages were checked against the other ages on the same page. The Rold Maren would have been 15 or 16 at that wedding.
  2. Both women were alive in different places in 1834. That year his mother was in Skelund, married to Christian Frederich Christensen. The Rold Maren was single: not in her parents' house, and most probably the unmarried maid of 29 at Blenstrup.
  3. She never married. Every record of her says so: the censuses of 1850 and 1860, Tre Slægter fra Rold ("Ugift", unmarried), and her burial entry ("Pige"). His mother married twice, in 1821 and in 1830.
  4. She had no child. Tre Slægter fra Rold calls her childless. She never appears with a child, and her burial names her by her brother, not by any son.
  5. Their lives ran in different places. The Rold Maren's life stayed around Rold and Blenstrup, in the middle of Himmerland. His mother's ran from Mou to Hurup in Als and on to Skelund, on the east coast of Hindsted Herred.

The consequence is that theory 3 in chapter 4, that the name Damborg came to him through his mother, can be set aside. The tree's 1879 death date and its birth year of 1778 for his mother should both be corrected. Her own death has not yet been found.

What is still uncertain

  • Her status in 1830. The word before her name in the Skelund marriage of 1830 is probably Enken (the widow), but a family reading gives Pigen or Jomfru (unmarried). This does not affect which Maren she was, since her age in 1834 settles that. It does affect when Ole Nielsen died, or whether the marriage ended some other way.
  • The Blenstrup maid. That she was the Rold Maren is likely but not proved. The 1840 or 1845 census, or Rold's moving-out lists for about 1830–34, would settle it.
  • Where his mother came from. Her birthplace and parents are unknown. The 1845 Skelund census, which gives birthplaces, and the Mou and Visborg moving-in lists for about 1819–21 are the next places to look.
